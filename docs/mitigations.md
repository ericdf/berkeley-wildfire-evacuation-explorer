## How it was run

Every lever runs against the same baseline: `DEFAULT_PLAN` (first order 10 minutes after ignition, status-quo parking), the Time Study's fall-night case S6, fires eastWind, ref1923, panoramic, northHills, quake, westWind, low/central/high severity × seeds 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 (15 same-seed pairs per lever and fire). A lever **holds** on a measure only if it improves that measure in all 15 pairs (the Simple-view rule, SOURCE_NOTES P19). Medians are central severity (5 runs). The change is the lever's central median minus the baseline's. A 90% clearance that is never reached counts as slower than any finished run, and a median that lands on such a run shows "not reached". 2100 simulator runs, 154.4 minutes on 4 workers.

Measures (all from `summary()` in `js/sim.js`):

- **People still there when embers arrived**: synthetic people at home (with or without a car, ordered or not) when embers reached their street, or on the road in a car under embers after leaving; each person counted once (SOURCE_NOTES FO8; the Simple-view headline, js/presets.js).
- **People still home when the fire front arrived**: synthetic people still at home when the fire front reached their street (summary().fireFrontHome); early leavers and bought-out households never count.
- **Cars cut off by fire**: vehicles on the road at the end of the run with no route to safety (summary().vehiclesNoRoute; the results panel's "vehicles cut off by fire").
- **Hill residents an engine cannot reach within 5 minutes**: synthetic Berkeley Fire Zones 2–3 residents whose street no engine reaches from a station within the City's 5-minute standard, at ignition, under the run's parked cars (summary().engineReachBeyond5People); the same on every fire for a seed.
- **Hill residents with no engine route**: synthetic Berkeley Fire Zones 2–3 residents whose street no engine route reaches at all from a Berkeley station, at ignition, under the run's parked cars and fixed barriers (summary().engineReachUnreachablePeople); added 2026-11-06 for the spread-and-access explorer's paired comparisons (SOURCE_NOTES SA1–SA12).
- **Structures lost in Fire Zones 2–3 (central)**: City footprints in Fire Zones 2–3 the fire front reached, each lost with a probability by its gap to the nearest one, cut by any home hardening or Zone 0 (summary().structuresLostCentral; js/structures.js; the midpoints of the assumption ranges and the central cut, SOURCE_NOTES PH23, PH32); the fire does not respond to structures. Added 2026-11-13; for runs saved before then it is copied from the same fire, severity and seed's baseline, since no earlier lever sets hardening or Zone 0.
- **90% clearance, model minutes (not a forecast)**: minutes from the first order until 90% of vehicles starting in Order zones have left the order area (summary().clearP90, the Time Study's ETE); null if never reached in the run, which counts as slower than any finished run.

## What each lever is, its cost basis, and what the simulator cannot measure

### Leave early on a Red Flag day

Half of hill households (Fire Zones 2–3) leave on the Red Flag warning, before any fire starts. Applied with js/presets.js leaveEarly Plan B delta.

- **Holds on:** eastWind: people still there when embers arrived, people still home when the fire front arrived, cars cut off by fire, 90% clearance, model minutes (not a forecast); ref1923: people still there when embers arrived, people still home when the fire front arrived, cars cut off by fire, 90% clearance, model minutes (not a forecast); panoramic: people still there when embers arrived, people still home when the fire front arrived; northHills: people still there when embers arrived, 90% clearance, model minutes (not a forecast); quake: people still there when embers arrived, people still home when the fire front arrived, cars cut off by fire; westWind: people still there when embers arrived, 90% clearance, model minutes (not a forecast).
- **Better in most pairs and never worse:** panoramic: cars cut off by fire (14 better, 1 equal); northHills: people still home when the fire front arrived (14 better, 1 equal), cars cut off by fire (10 better, 5 equal); westWind: people still home when the fire front arrived (13 better, 2 equal), cars cut off by fire (8 better, 7 equal).
- **Cost:** little capital cost; not priced. Little capital cost: a warning and outreach the City already runs. The real costs are households' time away from home on every Red Flag day, unpriced.
- **What the simulator can't measure:** Whether half of hill households would actually leave on a real Red Flag day, and whether they keep doing it after Red Flag days with no fire (warning fatigue). The simulator places the early leavers out of the area before ignition.

### Decide faster

First evacuation order 5 minutes after the fire starts instead of 10, as in today's plan. Applied with js/presets.js orderSooner Plan B delta (the preset compares 20 → 5 min; here the baseline is DEFAULT_PLAN's 10).

- **Holds on:** eastWind: none; ref1923: people still there when embers arrived; panoramic: people still there when embers arrived, people still home when the fire front arrived, cars cut off by fire; northHills: none; quake: people still home when the fire front arrived; westWind: none.
- **Better in most pairs and never worse:** eastWind: people still home when the fire front arrived (14 better, 1 equal), cars cut off by fire (13 better, 2 equal); ref1923: people still home when the fire front arrived (14 better, 1 equal), cars cut off by fire (13 better, 2 equal); northHills: people still home when the fire front arrived (13 better, 2 equal), cars cut off by fire (10 better, 5 equal).
- **Cost:** little capital cost; not priced. Little capital cost: staffing and authority to issue an order on first report. Orders that turn out to be unnecessary have a social cost, unpriced.
- **What the simulator can't measure:** Whether a fire can be confirmed and an order issued 5 minutes after ignition, and the cost of orders for fires that would have been caught. How fast alerts reach people is not modeled separately.

## Not run

- **R0: no added units in hill areas** (not comparable on today's population): R0 acts only under a future-growth scenario; on today's population (no growth) it changes nothing, so it is not comparable with these levers. Its results are in SOURCE_NOTES R1–R8 (with maximum growth, R0 lowers people still there when embers arrived in all 30 pairs).
- **Power shutoff** (not comparable on today's population): A hazard condition (scenario setting), not a mitigation: signals go dark and run as all-way stops. Its effect on the baseline and on contraflow is in SOURCE_NOTES CF14

## Limits

- All levers run at fall night (S6), when hill curbs are fullest; the Simple-view leave-early and parking questions use the same case, the order-timing question uses a weekday (S4). Weekday evacuation times in the simulator run 25–80 minutes fast against the Time Study (HANDOFF calibration table).
- Measures are model-wide. A lever that acts on one street (a link, a widening, a red curb) moves a model-wide count only a little even where it helps its own area; the per-area effects are in `docs/egress-candidates.md`, `docs/widening.md` and `docs/managed-retreat.md`.
- Engine reach is measured at ignition from parked cars, so it is the same on every fire for a seed. Engines only drive in this simulator: better reach does not change the fire or who is still there.
- Bought-out households make no trip and are not counted, so part of a buyout's drop in people still there is the removed people themselves (SOURCE_NOTES RT5).
- Costs are copied, not recomputed: timing and parking levers have little capital cost and are left unpriced (null), not zero.
