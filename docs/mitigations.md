Generated by `node tools/mitigations.mjs` on 2026-11-02; data in `data/model/mitigations.json`. Educational comparison on synthetic households and illustrative fires, not a prediction and not an official evacuation plan. "People still there when embers arrived" counts synthetic people at that moment; it is not a casualty count. Costs are screening ranges copied from earlier outputs, not estimates of a project.

## How it was run

Every lever runs against the same baseline: `DEFAULT_PLAN` (first order 10 minutes after ignition, status-quo parking), the Time Study's fall-night case S6, fires eastWind, ref1923, panoramic, northHills, low/central/high severity × seeds 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 (15 same-seed pairs per lever and fire). A lever **holds** on a measure only if it improves that measure in all 15 pairs (the Simple-view rule, SOURCE_NOTES P19). Medians are central severity (5 runs). The change is the lever's central median minus the baseline's. A 90% clearance that is never reached counts as slower than any finished run, and a median that lands on such a run shows "not reached". 1040 simulator runs, 38.1 minutes on 7 workers.

**Like for like:** 980 of 980 lever runs drew the same households, vehicles, mobilization delays, rides and non-resident counts as their baseline run (households that leave early or are bought out are left out of the check, as in `tests/plan-draws.mjs` and `tests/retreat-draws.mjs`). The control lever (baseline rerun) matched the baseline on every measure in 20 of 20 runs.

Measures (all from `summary()` in `js/sim.js`):

- **People still there when embers arrived**: synthetic people at home, on the road or without a car when embers reached their street, including people in zones never told to leave (the Simple-view headline; js/presets.js).
- **People still home when the fire front arrived**: synthetic people still at home when the fire front reached their street (summary().fireFrontHome); early leavers and bought-out households never count.
- **Cars cut off by fire**: vehicles on the road at the end of the run with no route to safety (summary().vehiclesNoRoute; the results panel's "vehicles cut off by fire").
- **Hill residents an engine cannot reach within 5 minutes**: synthetic Berkeley Fire Zones 2–3 residents whose street no engine reaches from a station within the City's 5-minute standard, at ignition, under the run's parked cars (summary().engineReachBeyond5People); the same on every fire for a seed.
- **90% clearance (min)**: minutes from the first order until 90% of vehicles starting in Order zones have left the order area (summary().clearP90, the Time Study's ETE); null if never reached in the run, which counts as slower than any finished run.

## What each lever is, its cost basis, and what the simulator cannot measure

### Leave early on a Red Flag day

Half of hill households (Fire Zones 2–3) leave on the Red Flag warning, before any fire starts. Applied with js/presets.js leaveEarly Plan B delta.

- **Holds on:** eastWind: people still there when embers arrived, 90% clearance (min); ref1923: people still there when embers arrived, people still home when the fire front arrived, cars cut off by fire, 90% clearance (min); panoramic: people still there when embers arrived, people still home when the fire front arrived, cars cut off by fire; northHills: people still there when embers arrived, people still home when the fire front arrived, 90% clearance (min).
- **Better in most pairs and never worse:** eastWind: people still home when the fire front arrived (14 better, 1 equal), cars cut off by fire (13 better, 2 equal); northHills: cars cut off by fire (10 better, 5 equal).
- **Cost:** little capital cost; not priced. Little capital cost: a warning and outreach the City already runs. The real costs are households' time away from home on every Red Flag day, unpriced.
- **What the simulator can't measure:** Whether half of hill households would actually leave on a real Red Flag day, and whether they keep doing it after Red Flag days with no fire (warning fatigue). The simulator places the early leavers out of the area before ignition.

### Decide faster

## Limits

- All levers run at fall night (S6), when hill curbs are fullest; the Simple-view leave-early and parking questions use the same case, the order-timing question uses a weekday (S4). Weekday evacuation times in the simulator run 25–80 minutes fast against the Time Study (HANDOFF calibration table).
- Measures are model-wide. A lever that acts on one street (a link, a widening, a red curb) moves a model-wide count only a little even where it helps its own area; the per-area effects are in `docs/egress-candidates.md`, `docs/widening.md` and `docs/managed-retreat.md`.
- Engine reach is measured at ignition from parked cars, so it is the same on every fire for a seed. Engines only drive in this simulator: better reach does not change the fire or who is still there.
- Bought-out households make no trip and are not counted, so part of a buyout's drop in people still there is the removed people themselves (SOURCE_NOTES RT5).
- Costs are copied, not recomputed: timing and parking levers have little capital cost and are left unpriced (null), not zero.
