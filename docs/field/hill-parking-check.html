Hill parking check: 22 blocks, evening

Prepared 2026-11-04 for the Berkeley Wildfire Evacuation Explorer. Every parking count the simulator uses comes from one daytime moment: the City's 2020 aerial photos, counted by Claude and not yet checked by a person. Parking at night is an assumption. These 22 blocks are the ones counted from the air: 5 streets you photographed earlier and 17 drawn at random from two-way hill blocks 70–220 m long. Counting them in person settles two things: whether the aerial counts are right, and how much fuller the blocks are at night.

When. A weeknight, Tuesday to Thursday, 9 to 11 p.m., when most residents are home: the simulator's fall-night case. Not on a holiday, a Cal home game night or a street-sweeping morning. If you can, repeat a few blocks on a weekday 1 to 3 p.m., the time of day the 2020 photos show, to separate a counting error from a day-and-night difference.
Where. Each block runs between the two cross streets named. "Path", "Walk" and "Steps" are footpaths; the block ends where the path meets the street. The coordinates are the middle of the block. The blocks are in driving order: the shortest route through all 22 on the City street network, one-way streets included, driving each block from one end to the other (about 18.9 km). Each leg link below opens that stretch in Google Maps; tap the next leg when you finish one.
Privacy. Photos help with counting, but only the counts are kept; frame them to avoid faces and license plates where you can.

Route

  • Leg 1: blocks 1–5
  • Leg 2: blocks 5–10
  • Leg 3: blocks 10–14
  • Leg 4: blocks 14–19
  • Leg 5: blocks 19–22

What to write down for each block

  • Side 1 / Side 2: parked cars on each side (pick either side as side 1; trucks and vans count as cars).
  • Pinches: places where cars on both sides leave about one car's width or less to drive through.
  • Corners: cars parked within about 20 ft (one car length) before a corner crosswalk, marked or not, on the side cars approach from, which state law has barred since 2024 (daylighting, Vehicle Code §22500(n)).
  • Signs / red curb: posted no-parking signs or red curb on the block, and whether cars are parked against them.
  • Notes: cars far from the curb or on the sidewalk, driveways or gates that limit parking, anything unusual.
