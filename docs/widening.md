Widths, costs and parcel counts are a screening estimate, not engineering, appraisal, environmental review or legal analysis.** Counts only: no parcel identifiers, owners, addresses or imagery are stored.

## Findings

- **A parking change gets engines through the pinches that matter most; widening adds little the simulator can see.** On the 10 pinches simulated, banning parking on just the pinch and widening it to 20 ft clear gave the same engine reach in every run. Two of them (Panoramic Wy #1, Oak Ridge Rd #7) changed the central median of residents beyond 5-minute engine reach; behind the rest, today's parking leaves no one beyond 5 minutes in the central runs, or a narrower street further in still blocks. Neither change moved evacuation behind any pinch beyond noise: the median change in the 90% time over central-severity pairs was between -0.1 and 0 minutes, with pairs going both ways. In an evacuation nearly all traffic drives out, and in the simulator a hill street carries one outbound lane whether it is 17 ft or 28 ft wide; the second lane serves engines and drivers coming in.
- **The lowest block of Panoramic Wy** (49 m, 17 ft of pavement by City data) is the top pinch: 952 Berkeley hill residents lose 5-minute engine reach if it closes to engines, and 972 synthetic residents live behind it (AC-155, SOURCE_NOTES AC14–AC15). One parked side leaves 9.1 ft, under the 10 ft an engine needs.
  - *Red curb on that block alone:* 17.1 ft clear, enough for an engine, not for two-way traffic (20 ft). About 7 parking spaces lost; $200–$600 in paint and signs.
  - *Widen for an engine, keeping the parked side:* 0.29 m (1 ft) to 18 ft; no parcels to acquire, 8 m² of retaining wall, $20k–$50k.
  - *Widen to 20 ft clear, keeping the parked side:* 3.34 m (11 ft) to 28 ft, laid on the right side; 156 of the 167 m² strip lies outside County parcels (likely public right of way, not verified), 1 parcel to acquire, no buildings in the new pavement and 1 within 3 m of it, 26 m² of retaining wall, $240k–$990k. Beside the block the ground rises 42% on one side and falls 7% on the other within 6 m of the pavement edge.
  - *Simulated* (East wind, Panoramic Hill, 1923 repeat fires; fall night; low, central and high severity × seeds 1–5): residents behind the block whom no engine reaches within 5 minutes, 953 → 9 central with either change, better in 12 of 15 same-seed pairs and equal in 3 (seed 4 draws no car on the block). Engine reach at ignition depends only on the seed's parked cars, so the three fires repeat the same five seeds. Across Berkeley's hills, residents beyond 5-minute reach fall 1,510 → 761; one-side parking on every hill street gives 1,010 (SOURCE_NOTES ER2), because it leaves the block's one parked side in place. In the Panoramic Hill fire, 322 synthetic people behind the block are still home when the fire front arrives with or without either change, and 131 of the area's vehicles never get out of it (130 widened, 130 with the red curb).
- **Where width binds.** With today's parking in its nominal state (both sides where a car still gets by, tight to the curb), 106 measured hill segments (12.5 km) leave less than the 10 ft an engine needs, and 741 (88.9 km) less than the 20 ft of the California Fire Code (§503.2.1). 66 binding pinches have people who depend on them. No measured hill pavement is under 10 ft. Another 661 hill segments outside Berkeley would bind on assumed widths and are left out.
- **What the top 30 would take.** A red curb alone gives 20 ft clear on 14 of them, where parked cars are what narrow a 20–32 ft street. On 9 nobody parks today (pavement 12–15 ft), so only widening changes anything. Widening all 30 to 20 ft clear with today's parking kept: 72 parcels to acquire (none on 13 pinches), 4 buildings in the new pavement and 16 within 3 m of it, $25M–$73M before engineering, environmental review and contingency.

## Method

Every rule below is a model rule unless it is marked as sourced or derived. Full wording is in `meta.rules` of the JSON.

- **Hill segments:** drivable street segments in Fire Zones 2–3 (City layer, or 90 m elevation and above beyond the city line) with a measured pavement width: City pavement data (`PAV_WIDTH_RD` or the Narrow_Roads survey) or an OpenStreetMap width tag. 1,709 hill segments have a width, 883 measured; Lab private streets are left out.
- **Nominal parking** (`js/parking.js`, no random draws): residents park both sides where a car still has 8 ft to pass, else one side, else none, tight to the curb, 8 ft per parked side (SB 99 p. 2–3; SOURCE_NOTES A1). The simulator runs add the seed's random parking (empty blocks, cars partly off the pavement, a bad parking job).
- **Binds:** clear width under 10 ft (engine; SB 99 p. 2–3, SOURCE_NOTES A2) or under 20 ft (two-way traffic; California Fire Code §503.2.1, `access.fireCodeClearM`).
- **Removal impact:** Berkeley hill residents (BER- zones on Fire Zone 2–3 streets, the population of the simulator's engine-reach measure, SOURCE_NOTES ER1) whose street an engine reaches within 5 minutes on a network with no parked cars and does not once the pinch alone is closed to engines. Engine rules as `js/sim.js` `engineTimes` at ignition: Berkeley's 7 stations, 1.15 × posted speed, gates 3 min, a home reached at the nearer end of its segment. Residents are the simulator's expected allocation. With today's nominal parking 1,268 of 27,835 are beyond 5 minutes; with no parked cars, 0.
- **Choke:** the pinch is an entrance of an access-constrained area (`data/model/access-constraints.json`, SOURCE_NOTES AC1–AC15); residents behind are that area's.
- **Score** = the larger of the two counts. Adjacent binding segments of one street that enter the same area or cut off the same residents (within 10%) form one pinch.
- **Standards:** *engine* = 10 ft clear plus 8 ft per side parked today; *fire code* = 20 ft unobstructed plus 8 ft per side parked today; *full* = two 10 ft lanes plus parking on both sides (36 ft, assumption).
- **Strip:** today's edge of pavement sits half the City width from the County centerline (assumption). The gain is laid out three ways (half each side, all left, all right); the table shows the layout with the fewest buildings in the new pavement, then within 3 m beyond it, then parcels, then retaining wall.
- **Parcels** (Alameda County parcel polygons, geometry only, never saved): parcels the strip enters, sampled every 1 m along and 0.25 m across; stacked condominium outlines count once; a parcel holding 1 m² or more of strip is a partial take. **Buildings** (OpenStreetMap footprints, never saved): those the new pavement reaches, and those within 3 m beyond it.
- **Side slope and walls** (4 m lidar DEM, bare earth): ground rise from today's edge to 6 m beyond, per side. A retaining wall where the ground 1 m beyond the new edge is 0.6 m (2 ft) or more above or below the centerline; wall face = that height along that length.
- **Parking-only alternative:** no parking on the pinch's segments (red curb). Spaces lost = curb length per parked side ÷ 6.7 m (22 ft, assumption).

## Cost inputs (all assumptions built on the cited figures)

- **Pavement:** $789–$1,578 per m² of added pavement. Low: Florida DOT Cost Per Mile Models, U19 "Add 2 Lanes to Existing 2 Lane Undivided Arterial (1 Lane Each Side), with 4' Bike Lanes", curb and gutter, sidewalk, drainage and lighting: $9,540,676.51 per mile (October 2024 update), with 14,467.2 SY of new base per mile (12,096 m², 7.5 m of added width): $789 per m² of added pavement, all items included, flat terrain, no right of way (http://web.archive.org/web/20251006210442/https://fdotwww.blob.core.windows.net/sitefinity/docs/default-source/fpo/fpc/reports/costpermile/urban/urban-19.pdf, retrieved 2026-11-01 (Wayback copy of 2025-10-06; listed on the cost-per-mile page also used by step 2)). High: twice that (assumption: a small hillside job in the Bay Area).
- **Retaining walls:** $753–$2,691 per m² of wall face. WSDOT Bridge Design Manual M 23-50.24 (June 2025), Appendix 12.3-A1 Structural Estimating Aids, scoping-level unit costs updated April 2025 (mobilization included; no sales tax, engineering or contingency): reinforced concrete retaining wall $70–$110 per sq ft of exposed face; soldier pile wall $125–$170; soldier pile tieback wall $175–$250 (https://wsdot.wa.gov/publications/manuals/fulltext/M23-50/Chapter12.pdf, retrieved 2026-11-01). Low = the reinforced concrete wall's low figure, high = the soldier pile tieback wall's high figure.
- **Acquisition:** a partial take at 10%–25% of the block group's median home value (± its margin of error) per parcel, and a building the new pavement reaches taken whole at that value, as in egress step 2 (branch `egress-candidates`, PR #15, `tools/egress-candidates.mjs`). American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year, table B25077 median value of owner-occupied homes, by block group (Census Reporter API); block group from TIGERweb, retrieved 2026-11-01. A 1–2 ft frontage strip is a small piece of a lot, so the partial-take share is the least supported input; severance, fences, driveways, walls and trees are what it stands for.
- **Red curb:** $1–$4 per foot of curb for paint and signs (assumption; no public unit cost could be verified).
- **Left out:** engineering and design, environmental review (CEQA), legal and appraisal costs, relocation benefits, utility poles and lines (common along these streets), drainage beyond the FDOT items, cost growth since the figures' dates, and contingency. Construction time is not estimated.

## Limits

- A screening estimate, not engineering or legal analysis. Whether a strip is public right of way, the real edge of pavement, utilities, drainage, trees, driveways and the cost of a taking need a survey, a title search and an appraisal.
- **Environmental review:** widening a hill street is a project under CEQA; an initial study, and an environmental impact report where slopes, trees, creeks or historic resources are affected, would come first. Time and cost are not estimated here.
- **Construction time** is not estimated; design, review, acquisition (eminent domain where an owner will not sell) and construction would take years, against days for a parking change.
- **Displacement:** a building the new pavement reaches is counted as taken whole; buildings within the setback are counted as affected, not priced.
- Widths come from the City pavement data, measured edge to edge; the County centerline may not run down the middle of the pavement, which moves the strip between sides.
- The removal impact counts Berkeley residents only, because the station layer holds Berkeley's 7 stations; Panoramic Hill's Oakland residents (zone BER-013 is a Berkeley zone, so they count) are in, other Oakland and Kensington hill residents are not.
- Engine speed is 1.15 × posted speed with no turn or grade delay (SOURCE_NOTES ER3), so the 5-minute measure mostly counts homes that parked cars cut off, not homes beyond the standard by distance.
- A widening keeps lanes as the builder assigns them (one each way on hill streets); its effect on traffic comes through clear width: two moving lanes at 20 ft clear, else one shared lane.
- The engine measure is taken at ignition with no traffic. What 20 ft adds for engines, passing evacuees driving out, shows in the simulator's responder time with traffic, which this comparison does not report.
- Four of the 30 screened pinches lie in block groups whose median home value is top-coded by the Census Bureau at $2,000,001, so acquisition there is understated.
- Only the fire-code widening was simulated. The 1 ft engine widening where one side is parked passes an engine exactly as the red curb does in the nominal state; with the random parking draws, a bad parking job can still block it.
- Synthetic households and illustrative fires: the people counts are synthetic and "still home at the fire front" is not a casualty count.
