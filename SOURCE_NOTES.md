# Source notes: what we learned from which document

Each entry records an insight, where it came from (document + section/page), and what we
did with it in the simulator. Keep adding entries as documents are read. Local copies of the
documents are in `data/raw/docs/`.

Status key: **applied** (changed the simulator), **pending** (planned change), **context**
(informs explanations or the assumptions panel only), **conflict** (contradicts something
we assumed; see the note).

| M1 | EMBER's levels of strictness are by distance from the house and by area. ## California ADU law and Berkeley's hill ADU rules (public sources, read 2026-11-01)

Researched for the R0 setting (Eric, 2026-11-01). Statute text was read on california.public.law's mirror of leginfo (leginfo.legislature.ca.gov refused automated requests); the mirror shows each section current to 2026. Inferences are marked as ours.

| # | Insight (source) | Status / use |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Area designation.** A local ADU ordinance may "Designate areas within the jurisdiction of the local agency where accessory dwelling units may be permitted. The designation of areas may be based on the adequacy of water and sewer services and the impact of accessory dwelling units on traffic flow and public safety." Gov. Code §66314(a) (https://california.public.law/codes/government_code_section_66314), same wording as former §65852.2(a)(1)(A); recodified by SB 477 (2024). §66315 makes §66314 the maximum standards; §66316 requires ministerial approval. | **applied**: R0 legal note, "what current law allows". |
| L2 | **By-right units.** "Notwithstanding Sections 66314 to 66322," a city "shall ministerially approve" on a single-family lot one ADU and one JADU within existing or proposed space, and one detached new-construction ADU of up to 800 sq ft with 4 ft setbacks (Gov. No fire or evacuation exception. | **applied**: R0 legal note. Our reading: the Time Study's 2 ADUs + 1 JADU per parcel (E26) is the §66323 combination, so all 27,060 units of its maximum are by-right units. |
| L3 | **HCD guidance.** ADU Handbook, March 2026 edition, pp. 42–43 (https://www.hcd.ca.gov/sites/default/files/docs/policy-and-research/adu-handbook-update.pdf): area limits "should be accompanied by detailed findings of fact"; Fire Hazard Severity Zone maps "are not intended to restrict housing development" and are "not a conclusive rationale"; designated areas "cannot preclude units described in Government Code section 66323"; limiting those needs a §66300(b)(1)(B) moratorium, "only with HCD's approval", against an "imminent threat to … health and safety". | **applied**: R0 legal note. |
| L4 | **HCD on Berkeley's 2022 hill limit.** Ord. 7,799-N.S. (BMC 12.99, Feb. 8, 2022) allowed one ADU or JADU per Hillside Overlay lot. The Time Study's ADU sensitivity (E26) is closer to that than the Wong study; HCD has not ruled on it (our inference). |
| L5 | **SB 9** (two units per lot, lot splits; Gov. Code §65852.21, §66411.7). Site exclusions incorporate §65913.4(a)(6)(B)–(K) as of Sept. 16, 2021, which excludes very high fire hazard severity zones (VHFHSZ) but not sites with fire hazard mitigation measures (Building Code ch. 7A, PRC §4290–4291); the 2021 wording that controls was not retrieved. The EO suspended SB 9 only in the Palisades and Eaton burn areas and was sued (YIMBY Law, LASC 25STCP04761). |
| L6 | **Housing Crisis Act** (Gov. Code §66300): bars "reducing the intensity of land use" below Jan. 1, 2018 zoning ((b)(1)(A)) and caps on units or population ((b)(1)(D)); allows a moratorium only for an imminent threat and with HCD approval ((b)(1)(B)); allows downzoning with "no net loss in residential capacity" elsewhere ((h)(1)). (e)(4): the section "shall not apply to a housing development project located within a very high fire hazard severity zone"; whether that covers a zoning ordinance, as opposed to a single project, is unresolved (no HCD or court reading found). | **applied**: R0 legal note (moratorium route). **Open question**: the (e)(4) reading. |
| L7 | **Bills, 2023–2026.** Chaptered: AB 1033 (2023), SB 477 (2024), SB 1211 (2024), AB 2533 (2024), and SB 543, AB 1154, SB 9 (2025) and AB 462 (2025, effective on signing). | L8 | **Berkeley's current hill rules**: Ord. 7,983-N.S. No count for the hills alone was found; the APR parcel lists could be tallied by zone without keeping addresses. | **applied**: moderate growth (2,706 units, 10% of the maximum) is about 25 years of the citywide pace if every ADU were in the hills (assumption, `growth.options.moderate`). |

**Bottom line for R0.** *What current law allows:* bar ADUs beyond the by-right minimum (more or larger §66314 units) in designated areas such as Fire Zones 2–3, the Leave Early Zones or access-constrained streets, with ADU-specific findings (L1, L3, L4); size, height and form limits like Ord. 7,983 (L8); case-by-case SB 9 denials on a specific adverse impact (L5); a moratorium covering by-right units with HCD's approval on an imminent-threat finding (L3, L6); and, our inference, base-zoning reductions offset by upzoning elsewhere (L6). *What would need a change in state law:* a standing area ban on §66323 by-right units (up to three per single-family lot, which is the whole of the Time Study's maximum); an area-wide SB 9 exclusion in Berkeley's VHFHSZ; a unit or population cap, unless §66300(e)(4) is read to cover ordinances. Additions that add floor area but no unit fall outside ADU and SB 9 law and add no household in the simulator; limiting them by floor area or lot coverage would count as reducing the intensity of land use under §66300(b)(1)(A), subject to the (e)(4) question.
