SubjectRequest for evacuation study data: curb parking, signal timing and fire access

Hello [name],

I'm a Berkeley Hills resident building a browser-based teaching tool that lets residents explore wildfire evacuation tradeoffs on Berkeley's streets: when zones are ordered, leaving early, parking on narrow hill streets, and fire-engine access. It is labeled as not a prediction and not an official plan, it links to the City's evacuation pages, and every number in it shows its source.

It uses the City's public GIS layers and the 2025 Evacuation Time Study's demand and mobilization tables. For Panoramic Hill its evacuation times come within 10 minutes of the study. For the larger regions it runs about 15 minutes fast at night and 35 to 80 minutes fast on weekdays. Four items would close most of the remaining gaps:

  1. Curb parking data that KLD used for the SB 99 and AB 747 analyses: where parking is allowed on one side, both sides or neither, and red curbs. I now estimate it from street photos and parked cars counted on the City's 2020 aerial photos, which show one daytime moment. The Narrow_Roads "width with parking" appears to be computed (7 or 8 ft per side), so any field measurements would also help.
  2. Traffic signal locations and timing in and near the hills (pre-timed or actuated, cycle lengths), or KLD's model network for the 2025 study. Signal timing is the most likely cause of the weekday gap.
  3. The City's list of traffic diverters and gates. I use OpenStreetMap's and would like to check it, especially the Panoramic Way gate and the Derby St diverter, which the study names as a constraint for Panoramic Hill.
  4. From Berkeley Fire: which hill streets engines have trouble getting through with normal parking, and, if it can be shared, on-duty staffing per shift and how off-duty crews are called back in a regional emergency.

I'm not asking for personal or household information. I'm happy to share the tool and its source notes with staff and to credit the City and KLD for anything you provide.

Thank you for any help, and for the work behind the evacuation study.
